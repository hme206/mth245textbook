Chapter 3: Introduction to Statistics

Section 3.6: Exercises

The following exercises review the major concepts introduced in this chapter. Show your work and simplify answers when appropriate.

Overview and Definitions

  1. What is the primary purpose of statistics?
    1. To eliminate uncertainty
    2. To collect, organize, analyze, interpret, and present data
    3. To prove that a hypothesis is true
    4. To study only numerical data
  2. Which of the following is not listed as a major goal of statistics?
    1. Evaluating evidence
    2. Identifying patterns
    3. Making informed decisions
    4. Eliminating all sampling variability
  3. Which of the following is an example of statistics being used in medicine?
    1. Measuring the effectiveness of a medication
    2. Designing a building
    3. Writing a computer program
    4. Translating a medical document
  4. Define a population in statistics.
  5. Define a sample.
  6. A researcher wants to determine the average age of all students at a university. She randomly selects 500 students and records their ages. Identify the:
    • Population
    • Sample
  7. A researcher collects information from every member of a population. Is this a census or a sample survey?
  8. A researcher collects information from 1,000 randomly selected members of a population. Is this a census or a sample survey?
  9. What is a parameter?
  10. What is a statistic?
  11. The average height of every student at a university is 68.4 inches. Is 68.4 a parameter or a statistic?
  12. The average height of 100 randomly selected students is 67.9 inches. Is 67.9 a parameter or a statistic?
  13. Match each symbol with its usual meaning: \[\mu,\quad \sigma,\quad \bar{x},\quad s\]
    1. Sample mean
    2. Population standard deviation
    3. Population mean
    4. Sample standard deviation
  14. What is the difference between categorical and quantitative data?
  15. Classify each variable as categorical or quantitative:
    1. Eye color
    2. Height
    3. Blood type
    4. Annual income
  16. A school records students' college majors. Is this categorical or quantitative data?
  17. A researcher records the number of children in each household. Is this categorical or quantitative data?
  18. Is hair color a qualitative or quantitative variable?
  19. Is weight a qualitative or quantitative variable?
  20. Explain the general relationship between qualitative variables and categorical data.
  21. What is the difference between a discrete and a continuous variable?
  22. Classify each variable as discrete or continuous:
    1. Number of students in a classroom
    2. Height of a student
    3. Number of defective products
    4. Temperature
  23. A stopwatch records a runner's time to complete a race. Is time discrete or continuous?
  24. Identify the level of measurement for each variable:
    1. Eye color
    2. Class rank
    3. Temperature in Celsius
    4. Weight
  25. Explain why \(20^\circ\mathrm{C}\) cannot meaningfully be described as “twice as warm” as \(10^\circ\mathrm{C}\).

Data Collection and Sampling

  1. What is a sampling frame?
  2. A university uses its student directory as the list from which it selects students for a survey. What is the sampling frame?
  3. A researcher assigns every student a number and uses a random-number generator to select 50 students. What sampling method is being used?
  4. A researcher surveys whichever students happen to be standing in the cafeteria. What sampling method is this?
  5. A researcher randomly selects a starting point on a customer list and then selects every 20th customer. What sampling method is this?
  6. A university divides students into freshmen, sophomores, juniors, and seniors and randomly selects students from each group. What sampling method is this?
  7. A researcher randomly selects five elementary schools and surveys every student in those schools. What sampling method is this?
  8. A survey requires 100 males and 100 females, and interviewers select people conveniently until both quotas are filled. What sampling method is this?
  9. A national survey randomly selects states, then counties within those states, then households within those counties. What type of sampling is this?
  10. What is undercoverage?
  11. An online-only survey about internet access excludes people without internet access. What type of problem could this create?
  12. What is sampling error?
  13. Which of the following is a nonsampling error?
    1. Different random samples produce different sample means
    2. A respondent misunderstands a survey question
    3. Random variation between samples
    4. A larger sample produces a slightly different statistic

Experimental Design

  1. What is an observational study?
  2. What is an experiment?
  3. Researchers record the smoking habits and lung health of 5,000 people without assigning anyone to smoke or not smoke. Is this an observational study or an experiment?
  4. Researchers randomly assign patients to receive either a new medication or the current medication. Is this an observational study or an experiment?
  5. Why are observational studies generally unable to establish cause-and-effect relationships?
  6. What is a treatment group?
  7. What is a control group?
  8. Researchers compare two exercise programs, but participants in one program are substantially younger than participants in the other. Why might age be a confounding variable?
  9. What is the purpose of random assignment in an experiment?
  10. A researcher divides students into groups based on grade level and then randomly assigns students within each grade level to one of two teaching methods. What experimental design is this?
    1. Completely randomized design
    2. Randomized block design
    3. Convenience design
    4. Cluster sampling
  11. Identify the effect described in each situation.
    1. Participants report improvement because they believe they received an effective treatment.
    2. Employees change their behavior because they know they are being observed.
    3. Researchers unintentionally influence participants because of their expectations.
    Choose from:
    • Placebo effect
    • Hawthorne effect
    • Rosenthal effect
  12. What is the difference between a single-blind and a double-blind experiment?
