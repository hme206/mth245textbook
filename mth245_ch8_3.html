Chapter 8: Estimation

Section 8.3: Student's t-Distribution

In practice, the population standard deviation is rarely known. Instead, it is estimated using the sample standard deviation, \(s\).

The \(t\) Confidence Interval

Definition: Student's \(t\) Distribution

Replacing \(\sigma\) with \(s\) introduces additional uncertainty. To account for this uncertainty, the Student's \(t\) distribution is used.

For a one-sample \(t\) confidence interval, the observations should come from an appropriate random sampling process and should be independent. For small samples, the population distribution should be approximately normal, with no severe outliers.

The \(t\) distribution has a shape similar to the standard normal distribution but has heavier tails. As the sample size increases, the \(t\) distribution approaches the standard normal distribution. The exact shape of the \(t\)-distribution depends on the degrees of freedom.

The confidence interval becomes

\[\bar{x}\pm t_{\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\]

where

\[t_{\alpha/2,n-1}\]

is obtained from the Student's \(t\) distribution using \(n-1\) degrees of freedom.

Example 1

Suppose \(n=16\), \(\bar{x}=74\), and \(s=8\). Construct a 95% confidence interval.

Solution

The degrees of freedom are \(n-1=15\). From the \(t\) table, \(t_{0.025,15}\approx2.131\).

The margin of error is

\[E=2.131\left(\frac{8}{4}\right)=4.262.\]

Thus, \(74\pm4.262\), or \((69.738,\;78.262)\).

We are \(95\%\) confident that the true population mean lies between 69.74 and 78.26, or more formally, in repeated sampling, approximately 95% of intervals constructed by this procedure will contain the true mean.
