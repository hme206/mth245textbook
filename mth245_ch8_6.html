Chapter 8: Estimation

Section 8.6: Summary

A confidence interval provides a range of plausible values for an unknown population parameter. It combines a point estimate with a margin of error and is associated with a specified confidence level. Higher confidence levels produce wider intervals, while larger sample sizes generally produce narrower intervals.

For a population mean with known standard deviation, the standard normal distribution is used. The margin of error is determined by the critical value, population standard deviation, and sample size. For a one-sample population mean when the standard deviation is unknown and the assumptions for the \(t\)-procedure are satisfied, the Student's \(t\)-distribution with \(n-1\) degrees of freedom is used.

Confidence intervals can also be constructed for population proportions. The sample proportion \(\hat{p}\) is used as the point estimate, provided the sample is sufficiently large for the normal approximation to be appropriate.

Sometimes only an upper or lower limit is needed. In these cases, one-sided confidence bounds provide an appropriate estimate and use a critical value based on a single tail of the distribution.

Overall, the appropriate confidence interval procedure depends on the parameter being estimated, whether the population standard deviation is known, and whether a two-sided interval or one-sided bound is required.
