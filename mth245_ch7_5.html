Chapter 7: Normal Distribution

Section 7.5: Central Limit Theorem

One of the most important concepts in statistics is the Central Limit Theorem (CLT). It explains why the normal distribution appears so frequently in statistics, even when the original population is not normally distributed.

The Central Limit Theorem

Definition: Sampling Distribution

The Central Limit Theorem describes the behavior of the sampling distribution of the sample mean. A sampling distribution is the probability distribution of a statistic obtained from repeated samples of a fixed size drawn according to the specified sampling process.

Suppose a population has mean \(\mu\) and standard deviation \(\sigma\). If many random samples of size \(n\) are selected from the population and the mean of each sample is computed, then these sample means form a new distribution.

The Central Limit Theorem

The Central Limit Theorem states that, under appropriate conditions, including independence and a sufficiently large sample size, the sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normal even when the original population is not normally distributed. Furthermore,

\[\mu_{\bar{x}}=\mu, \quad\text{and}\quad \sigma_{\bar{x}}=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}},\]

where \(\mu_{\bar{x}}\) is the mean of the sampling distribution and \(\sigma_{\bar{x}}\) is called the standard error of the mean.

Notice that the standard error decreases as the sample size increases. As sample size increases, the standard error decreases, so sample means tend to vary less from sample to sample around the population mean.

When Does the Central Limit Theorem Apply?

The Central Limit Theorem applies under the following conditions.

  • The sample is selected randomly.
  • The observations are independent.
  • The sample size is sufficiently large.
  • When sampling without replacement from a finite population, a common guideline is that the sample size should be no more than about 10% of the population so that observations can be treated as approximately independent.

A commonly used guideline is:

  • If the population is approximately normal, the theorem works well for almost any sample size.
  • If the population is not normal, a sample size of \(n\ge30\) is often sufficient for the sampling distribution of the mean to be approximately normal.

There is no universal sample-size cutoff that guarantees normality of the sampling distribution. A common introductory rule of thumb is \(n\geq30\), but the required sample size depends on the shape of the population distribution; strongly skewed or heavy-tailed populations may require much larger samples.

Example 1

Suppose the weights of packages have \(\mu=10\) lb and \(\sigma=2\) lb. Random samples of \(n=25\) packages are selected. Find the mean and standard error of the sampling distribution of the sample mean.

Solution

The sampling distribution of the sample mean has

\[\mu_{\bar{x}}=10\]

and

\[\sigma_{\bar{x}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{25}}=\frac{2}{5}=0.4.\]

Although individual package weights vary with a standard deviation of 2 lb, the sample means vary by only 0.4 lb.

Example 2

A population has \(\mu=80\) and \(\sigma=15\). Find the mean and standard error of the sampling distribution when \(n=100\).

Solution

Since

\[\mu_{\bar{x}}=\mu,\]

we have

\[\mu_{\bar{x}}=80.\]

The standard error is

\[\sigma_{\bar{x}}=\frac{15}{\sqrt{100}}=\frac{15}{10}=1.5.\]

Therefore,

\[\bar{x}\sim N(80,1.5^2)\]

approximately, provided the sample size is large enough or the original population is approximately normal.

Why the Central Limit Theorem Matters

The Central Limit Theorem allows statisticians to use the normal distribution to study sample means, even when the original population is not normally distributed. This theorem provides an important theoretical basis for many statistical procedures involving sample means, including procedures that use normal or approximately normal sampling distributions.

Without the Central Limit Theorem, many of the statistical methods used in science, engineering, medicine, business, and the social sciences would not be possible.

Key Points

  • The Central Limit Theorem describes the distribution of sample means.
  • For sufficiently large samples, the sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normal.
  • The sampling distribution has mean \(\mu_{\bar{x}}=\mu.\)
  • The sampling distribution has standard deviation \(\sigma_{\bar{x}}=\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\), called the standard error of the mean.
  • Increasing the sample size decreases the standard error.
  • The Central Limit Theorem provides the foundation for confidence intervals and hypothesis testing.
