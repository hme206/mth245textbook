Chapter 2: Binomial Probability Distribution

Section 2.5: Summary

Random variables assign numerical values to the outcomes of random experiments. They may be discrete, with countable values, or continuous, with values over an interval. Discrete variables use probability mass functions (PMFs), while continuous variables use probability density functions (PDFs).

For a discrete random variable, the expected value represents the long-run average, while variance and standard deviation measure the spread of the values around the mean. Linear transformations change the mean and variance according to specific rules, and the means and variances of independent random variables can be combined.

A binomial distribution models a fixed number of independent trials with two possible outcomes and a constant probability of success. The binomial probability formula determines the probability of obtaining a specific number of successes, while the mean and standard deviation can be calculated using \(\mu=np\) and \(\sigma=\sqrt{npq}\).

Binomial probability tables provide a convenient way to find individual probabilities. More complex probabilities, such as “at most,” “at least,” or “between,” can be calculated by adding appropriate probabilities or using the complement rule.
