Chapter 2: Binomial Probability Distribution

Section 2.1: Introduction

Overview

This chapter introduces random variables, probability distributions, and the binomial distribution. It explains how random variables can be discrete or continuous and how probabilities are represented using probability mass functions and probability density functions. It develops key measures such as expected value, variance, and standard deviation before applying these concepts to binomial experiments and probability calculations.

Learning Objectives

After completing this chapter, you should be able to:

  • Define a random variable and distinguish between discrete and continuous random variables.
  • Identify and interpret probability mass functions (PMFs) and probability density functions (PDFs).
  • Calculate and interpret the mean (expected value), variance, and standard deviation of a random variable.
  • Apply rules for the mean and variance of linear transformations and combinations of independent random variables.
  • Identify the conditions required for a binomial experiment.
  • Use the binomial probability formula to calculate probabilities of obtaining a specified number of successes.
  • Interpret probability statements such as exactly, at most, at least, more than, and between.
  • Calculate the mean, variance, and standard deviation of a binomial random variable.
  • Use binomial probability tables to find individual and combined probabilities.
  • Apply the complement rule to simplify binomial probability calculations.
