Chapter 8: Estimation

Section 8.7: Exercises

The following exercises review the major concepts introduced in this chapter. Show your work and simplify answers when appropriate.

Point Estimates

  1. A random sample of 50 students has a mean test score of \(\bar{x}=84\). Find the point estimate for the population mean \(\mu\).
  2. In a survey of 200 students, 126 report that they regularly use a particular study app. Find the point estimate for the population proportion \(p\).
  3. A random sample of 40 batteries has a mean lifetime of 18.6 hours. Find the point estimate for the population mean lifetime.
  4. In a sample of 500 voters, 315 support a proposed policy. Find the point estimate for the population proportion.
  5. A sample of 64 students has a mean height of 68.4 inches. What statistic is used as the point estimate for the population mean height?

Margin of Error

Use \(E=z_{\alpha/2}\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\) when the population standard deviation is known.

  1. Find the margin of error for a 95% confidence level when \(\sigma=10\) and \(n=100\).
  2. Find the margin of error for a 90% confidence level when \(\sigma=18\) and \(n=64\).
  3. Find the margin of error for a 99% confidence level when \(\sigma=25\) and \(n=400\).
  4. A population has known standard deviation \(\sigma=12\). Find the margin of error for a 95% confidence level using a sample of \(n=36\).
  5. A researcher wants a 95% confidence interval with a margin of error no greater than \(2\). The population standard deviation is known to be \(\sigma=10\). What minimum sample size is required?

Confidence Intervals with Known \(\sigma\)

Use \(\bar{x}\pm z_{\alpha/2}\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}.\)

  1. A random sample of 64 students has \(\bar{x}=82\) and known \(\sigma=12\). Construct a 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\).
  2. A random sample of 50 adult men has \(\bar{x}=71\) inches and known \(\sigma=2.5\) inches. Construct a 95% confidence interval for the population mean height.
  3. A sample of 100 machines has a mean output of \(\bar{x}=105\) units per hour. The population standard deviation is known to be \(\sigma=15\). Construct a 99% confidence interval.
  4. A sample of 36 batteries has mean lifetime \(\bar{x}=48\) hours and known \(\sigma=8\) hours. Construct a 90% confidence interval.
  5. A sample of 225 measurements has \(\bar{x}=210\) and known \(\sigma=30\). Construct a 95% confidence interval.

\(t\)-Intervals

Students should determine when the Student's \(t\) distribution is appropriate, find the degrees of freedom, select the critical value, and construct the interval.

  1. A sample of 16 observations has \(\bar{x}=74\) and \(s=8\). The population standard deviation is unknown. Construct a 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\). State the degrees of freedom and the appropriate \(t\) critical value.
  2. A sample of 10 observations has \(\bar{x}=52\) and \(s=6\). Construct a 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\). State the degrees of freedom and the appropriate \(t\) critical value.
  3. A sample of 25 observations has \(\bar{x}=101\) and \(s=12\). Construct a 99% confidence interval for \(\mu\).
  4. A sample of 20 observations has \(\bar{x}=18.5\) and \(s=2.5\). Construct a 90% confidence interval for \(\mu\).
  5. A sample of 30 observations has \(\bar{x}=65\) and \(s=5\). Construct a 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\).

Choosing \(z\) versus \(t\)

For each situation, determine whether a \(z\)-interval or a \(t\)-interval for a population mean is appropriate. Explain briefly.

  1. A random sample of 64 students has \(\bar{x}=78\). The population standard deviation is known to be \(\sigma=10\).
  2. A random sample of 18 students has \(\bar{x}=74\) and \(s=7\). The population standard deviation is unknown, and the population is approximately normal.
  3. A random sample of 100 measurements has \(\bar{x}=42.6\). The population standard deviation is known to be \(\sigma=5\).
  4. A random sample of 12 measurements has \(\bar{x}=31.4\) and \(s=4.1\). The population standard deviation is unknown, and the population is approximately normal with no severe outliers.
  5. A random sample of 45 observations has \(\bar{x}=105\) and \(s=12\). The population standard deviation is unknown. Which procedure should be used: \(z\) or \(t\)? Explain why.

Proportion Intervals

Use the normal approximation interval \(\hat p\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\dfrac{\hat p\hat q}{n}},\ \hat q=1-\hat p\) only when the normal approximation is appropriate. Use the guideline \(\hat p n\ge5\) and \((1-\hat p)n\ge5\).

  1. In a sample of 400 voters, 240 support a proposal. Construct a 95% confidence interval for the population proportion.
  2. In a sample of 80 windshields, 74 are acceptable. Determine whether the normal approximation is appropriate. Then, if appropriate, construct a 99% confidence interval.
  3. In a sample of 30 customers, 18 report being satisfied. Determine whether the normal approximation is appropriate and construct a 95% confidence interval if it is appropriate.
  4. In a sample of 100 products, only 6 are defective. Determine whether the normal approximation is appropriate. Explain the limitation of using the traditional normal/Wald interval.
  5. In a sample of 40 patients, only 2 experience a particular outcome. Determine whether the normal approximation is appropriate. Explain why a normal-based interval may be unreliable in this case.

Confidence-Level Changes

For a fixed sample, compare 90%, 95%, and 99% confidence levels.

  1. A sample has \(\bar{x}=100\), \(\sigma=20\), and \(n=100\). Find the margin of error for 90%, 95%, and 99% confidence. Which confidence level produces the widest interval?
  2. Explain what happens to the width of a confidence interval when the confidence level changes from 90% to 95%, while the sample and variability remain fixed.
  3. A researcher reports a 95% confidence interval of \((48,52)\). Without calculating a new interval, explain whether a 99% confidence interval based on the same data would generally be narrower, the same width, or wider.
  4. For the same sample, rank the expected margins of error from smallest to largest for 90%, 95%, and 99% confidence.
  5. A researcher wants a narrower confidence interval but does not want to change the sample size. Should the researcher increase or decrease the confidence level? Explain the tradeoff.

Sample-Size Effects

For fixed confidence level and variability, \(E\propto\dfrac{1}{\sqrt n}.\)

  1. If the sample size is doubled, by what factor is the margin of error multiplied?
  2. If the sample size is quadrupled, by what factor is the margin of error multiplied?
  3. A study has a margin of error of \(4\) with sample size \(n=100\). What will the margin of error be approximately if the sample size is increased to \(400\)?
  4. A study has a margin of error of \(3\) with sample size \(n=200\). What will the margin of error be approximately if the sample size is reduced to \(50\)?
  5. A researcher wants to cut the margin of error in half without changing the confidence level or population variability. By what factor must the sample size be changed?

Confidence-Interval Interpretation

Use the frequentist interpretation emphasized in this chapter: the confidence level describes the long-run proportion of similarly constructed intervals that contain the true parameter.

  1. A 95% confidence interval for a population mean is \((67.8,69.0)\). Write a standard interpretation of this interval.
  2. A student says, “There is a 95% probability that the population mean is between 67.8 and 69.0.” Explain why this is not the standard frequentist interpretation of a 95% confidence interval.
  3. A student says, “95% of the individual observations in the population are inside my confidence interval.” Explain why this statement is incorrect.
  4. A researcher constructs 100 confidence intervals using the same procedure and random sampling method. What does a 95% confidence level mean in terms of the long-run behavior of these intervals?
  5. A 99% confidence interval for a population proportion is \((0.42,0.58)\). Write a correct interpretation and explain what the 99% does not mean.

Confidence Bounds

Use one-sided critical values for confidence bounds:

\[\text{Lower Bound}=\bar{x}-z_\alpha\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\qquad \text{Upper Bound}=\bar{x}+z_\alpha\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}.\]
  1. A sample of 50 adult men has \(\bar{x}=71\) inches and known \(\sigma=2.5\) inches. Find a 95% upper confidence bound for the population mean height.
  2. A sample of 64 students has \(\bar{x}=100\) and known \(\sigma=12\). Find a 90% lower confidence bound for the population mean.
  3. A sample of 100 measurements has \(\bar{x}=50\) and known \(\sigma=10\). Find a 99% upper confidence bound.
  4. A random sample of 400 voters contains 240 supporters of a proposal. Find a 95% lower confidence bound for the population proportion.
  5. Explain the difference between a 95% two-sided confidence interval and a 95% one-sided upper confidence bound. Include the critical values used for the normal distribution.

Percentile vs. Confidence-Bound Questions

These questions distinguish a statement about the distribution of individual observations from a statement about an unknown population parameter.

  1. Adult male heights are normally distributed with mean \(\mu=70\) inches and standard deviation \(\sigma=3\) inches. Find the 95th percentile of the distribution of individual heights.
  2. Adult male heights have known population standard deviation \(\sigma=2.5\) inches. A random sample of 50 men has \(\bar{x}=71\) inches. Find a 95% upper confidence bound for the population mean.
  3. Explain the difference between these two questions: <blockquote>

    “Find the 95th percentile of individual heights.”

    </blockquote> and <blockquote>

    “Find a 95% upper confidence bound for the population mean height.”

    </blockquote>
  4. A population has individual measurements distributed as \(N(100,15^2)\). Find the 90th percentile for an individual observation. Is this a confidence interval or a percentile calculation?
  5. A random sample of \(n=64\) observations has \(\bar{x}=103\) and known \(\sigma=15\). Find a 95% upper confidence bound for the population mean. Explain why the resulting value should not be described as the 95th percentile of individual observations.
