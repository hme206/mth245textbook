Chapter 10: Advanced Hypothesis Testing

Section 10.1: Introduction

Overview

Many statistical questions involve comparing two populations rather than making an inference about a single population. For example, we may want to determine whether a new teaching method is more effective than a traditional method, whether a treatment reduces blood pressure, or whether two populations have different proportions.

In these situations, the parameter of interest is a difference:

\[\text{Difference}=\text{Parameter}_1-\text{Parameter}_2.\]

or

\[\mu_1-\mu_2 \quad\text{and}\quad p_1-p_2\]

The order of the populations matters. If Population 1 and Population 2 are switched, the sign of the difference changes.

This chapter considers three important cases:

  1. difference of means for matched pairs,
  2. difference of means for unmatched (independent) samples, and
  3. difference of two population proportions.

For each case, we consider both confidence intervals and hypothesis tests.

Learning Objectives

After completing this chapter, students should be able to:

  • Identify whether samples are matched or independent.
  • Construct confidence intervals for differences in means and proportions.
  • Perform hypothesis tests for matched-pairs means.
  • Perform hypothesis tests for two independent means.
  • Distinguish between matched-pairs and independent-samples designs.
  • Perform hypothesis tests for differences between two proportions.
  • Interpret the sign and meaning of a difference between two population parameters.
  • Interpret confidence intervals, test statistics, and \(p\)-values in context.
  • Select the appropriate statistical procedure for comparing two populations.
