Chapter 7: Normal Distribution

Section 7.4: Applications

Example 1

Consider a standard normal distribution. Approximately what percentage of data falls within the domain \(z<1.24\)?

Solution

Using the standard normal table,

\[P(Z<1.24)=0.8925.\]

Therefore, approximately \(89.25\%\) of the data falls below \(z=1.24\).

Example 2

Consider a standard normal distribution. Approximately what percentage of data falls within the domain \(z>1.86\)?

Solution

Using the standard normal table,

\[P(Z<1.86)=0.9686.\]

The area to the right of \(z=1.86\) is

\[P(Z>1.86)=1-0.9686=0.0314.\]

Therefore, approximately \(3.14\%\) of the data falls within the domain \(z>1.86\).

Example 3

Consider a standard normal distribution. Approximately what percentage of data falls within the domain \(0.53<z<3.02\)?

Solution

Using the standard normal table,

\[P(Z<3.02)=0.9987\]

and

\[P(Z<0.53)=0.7019.\]

Therefore,

\[P(0.53<Z<3.02)=0.9987-0.7019=0.2968,\]

so approximately \(29.68\%\) of the data falls within the domain \(0.53<z<3.02\).

Example 4

What z-score represents the bottom \(70\%\) of the population? What z-score represents the top \(5\%\) of the population?

Solution

For the bottom \(70\%\) of the population, we find the \(z\)-score such that \(P(Z<z)=0.70\). Using the standard normal table, the value falls between

\[z\approx0.52 \text{ and } z\approx0.53.\]

Since \(P(Z<z)=0.70\) falls between those values, we can round or find a linear approximation. In this text, we will round or approximate the value. Therefore, since the table gives \(z=0.52\) and \(z=0.53\) as the closest tabulated values, interpolating gives approximately \(z=0.525\).

For the top \(5\%\) of the population, we find the \(z\)-score with \(95\%\) of the data below it, or \(P(Z<z)=0.95\).

Using the standard normal table, the value falls between

\[z\approx1.64 \text{ and } z\approx1.65.\]

Therefore, since the table gives \(z=1.64\) and \(z=1.65\) as the closest tabulated values, interpolating gives approximately \(z=1.645\). This percentage is so common we typically use \(z\approx1.645\).

Example 5

Consider a normal distribution with \(\mu = 100\) and \(\sigma = 15\). Approximately what percentage of data falls within the domain \(x<120\)?

Solution

First, convert the value \(x=120\) to a \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}=\frac{120-100}{15}=\frac{20}{15}\approx1.33.\]

Using the standard normal table,

\[P(Z<1.33)=0.9082.\]

Therefore, approximately \(90.82\%\) of the data falls below \(x=120\).

Example 6

Consider a normal distribution with \(\mu = 100\) and \(\sigma = 15\). Approximately what percentage of data falls within the domain \(x>125\)?

Solution

First, convert the value \(x=125\) to a \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}=\frac{125-100}{15}=\frac{25}{15}\approx1.67.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<1.67)=0.9525\), but this is the area to the left. To find the area to the right, \(P(Z>1.67)=1-0.9525=0.0475\). Therefore, approximately \(4.75\%\) of the data falls above \(x=125\).

Example 7

Consider a normal distribution with \(\mu = 100\) and \(\sigma = 15\). Approximately what percentage of data falls within the domain \(105<x<135\)?

Solution

First, convert the endpoints to \(z\)-scores. For \(x=105\),

\[z=\frac{105-100}{15}=\frac{5}{15}\approx0.33.\]

For \(x=135\),

\[z=\frac{135-100}{15}=\frac{35}{15}\approx2.33.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<2.33)=0.9901\) and \(P(Z<0.33)=0.6293\). Therefore,

\[P(105<X<135)=P(0.33<Z<2.33)=0.9901-0.6293=0.3608.\]

Thus, approximately \(36.08\%\) of the data falls within the domain \(105<x<135\).

Example 8

Consider a normal distribution with \(\mu = 100\) and \(\sigma = 15\). What score represents the bottom \(70\%\) of the population? What score represents the top \(5\%\) of the population?

Solution

For the bottom \(70\%\) of the population, we first find the corresponding \(z\)-score.

\[P(Z<z)=0.70\]

Using the standard normal table, \(z\approx0.525.\)

Convert this to an \(x\)-value using

\[x=\mu+z\sigma=100+(0.525)(15)=107.875\approx108.\]

Therefore, the score representing the bottom \(70\%\) of the population is approximately \(108.\)

For the top \(5\%\) of the population, we need the score with \(95\%\) of the data below it.

\[P(Z<z)=0.95.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(z\approx1.645\).

Converting to an \(x\)-value,

\[x=100+(1.645)(15)=124.675\approx125.\]

Therefore, the score representing the top \(5\%\) of the population is approximately \(125\).

Example 9

The battery life of a certain brand of laptop follows a normal distribution with mean 16 hours and standard deviation 5 hours. A particular battery has a life of 10.2 hours.

  1. How many standard deviations below the mean is the battery life?
  2. What is the probability that a randomly chosen battery has a life shorter than this?

Solution

First, compute the \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}=\frac{10.2-16}{5}=\frac{-5.8}{5}=-1.16.\]

Thus, the battery life is \(1.16\) standard deviations below the mean.

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<-1.16)=0.1230\). Therefore, the probability that a randomly chosen battery has a life shorter than 10.2 hours is approximately \(0.123\).

Example 10

Weights of a certain breed of dog follow a normal distribution with mean 16 kg and variance 16 kg2. In a sample of 2000 such dogs, estimate the number that will have a weight above 13 kg.

Solution

The variance is \(16\text{ kg}^2\), so the standard deviation is \(\sigma=\sqrt{16}=4\text{ kg}\).

Next, convert \(x=13\) to a \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}=\frac{13-16}{4}=-0.75.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<-0.75)=0.2266\). Therefore,

\[P(X>13)=P(Z>-0.75)=1-0.2266=0.7734.\]

The expected number of dogs weighing more than \(13\) kg is

\[2000(0.7734)=1546.8\approx1547.\]

Thus, approximately 1547 dogs are expected to weigh more than \(13\) kg.

Example 11

The wingspan of a species of pigeon is normally distributed with mean 60 cm and standard deviation 6 cm. A pigeon is selected at random.

  1. Find the probability that its wingspan is greater than 50 cm.
  2. Given that this pigeon's wingspan is greater than 50 cm, find the probability that it is greater than 55 cm.

Solution

The wingspans are normally distributed with

\[\mu=60\text{ cm}, \qquad \sigma=6\text{ cm}.\]

For part (a), convert \(x=50\) to a \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{50-60}{6}=\frac{-10}{6}\approx-1.67.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<-1.67)=0.0475\).

Therefore, \(P(X>50)=1-0.0475=0.9525\) and \(P(X>50)\approx0.9525\).

For part (b), we want the conditional probability

\[P(X>55\mid X>50)=\frac{P(X>55)}{P(X>50)},\]

since every pigeon with a wingspan greater than \(55\) cm also has a wingspan greater than \(50\) cm.

First, convert \(x=55\) to a \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{55-60}{6}=\frac{-5}{6}\approx-0.83.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<-0.83)=0.2033\), so \(P(X>55)=1-0.2033=0.7967\).

From conditional probability,

\[P(X>55\mid X>50)=\frac{0.7967}{0.9525}\approx0.8364.\]

Example 12

Grains of sand are believed to have normally distributed widths of mean 2 mm and variance 0.25 mm2.

  1. Find the probability that a randomly chosen grain of sand is wider than 1.5 mm.
  2. The sand is passed through a filter which blocks grains wider than 2.5 mm. What is the probability that a randomly chosen grain of filtered sand is wider than 1.5 mm?

Solution

The grain widths are normally distributed with

\[\mu=2\text{ mm}, \qquad \sigma^2=0.25\text{ mm}^2.\]

Therefore, \(\sigma=\sqrt{0.25}=0.5\text{ mm}\).

For part (a), convert \(x=1.5\) to a \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{1.5-2}{0.5}=-1.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<-1)=0.1587\).

Therefore, \(P(X>1.5)=1-0.1587=0.8413\).

For part (b), we filter, and grains wider than 2.5 mm are removed. We need the probability that a remaining grain is wider than 1.5 mm.

This is a conditional probability:

\[P(X>1.5\mid X<2.5)=\frac{P(1.5<X<2.5)}{P(X<2.5)}.\]

Convert \(x=2.5\) to a \(z\)-score.

\[z=\frac{2.5-2}{0.5}=1.\]

Using the standard normal table, \(P(Z<1)=0.8413\).

The probability between \(1.5\) mm and \(2.5\) mm is

\[P(-1<Z<1)=0.8413-0.1587=0.6826.\]

Therefore,

\[P(X>1.5\mid X<2.5)=\frac{0.6826}{0.8413}\approx0.8114.\]
