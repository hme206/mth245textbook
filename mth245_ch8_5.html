Chapter 8: Estimation

Section 8.5: Confidence Bounds

Sometimes only a lower bound or an upper bound is required instead of an interval.

Lower and Upper Confidence Bounds

Definition: Lower Confidence Bound

A lower confidence bound provides a value below which the population parameter is unlikely to fall, according to the specified confidence procedure.

Definition: Upper Confidence Bound

An upper confidence bound provides a value above which the population parameter is unlikely to fall, according to the specified confidence procedure.

Formulas for Confidence Bounds

For a population mean with known standard deviation,

\[\text{Lower Bound}=\bar{x}-z_\alpha\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\]

and

\[\text{Upper Bound}=\bar{x}+z_\alpha\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}.\]

For a population proportion,

\[\text{Lower Bound}=\hat p-z_{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\hat p\hat q}{n}},\]

and

\[\text{Upper Bound}=\hat p+z_{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\hat p\hat q}{n}}.\]

Notice that one-sided confidence bounds use \(z_\alpha\) instead of \(z_{\alpha/2}\), because all of the allowable error is placed in only one tail of the distribution.

Care must be taken when finding the critical values. For example, a two-sided 95% interval yields

\[\alpha=0.05,\qquad z_{\alpha/2}=z_{0.025}=1.96\]

while a one-sided 95% bound yields

\[\alpha=0.05,\qquad z_\alpha=z_{0.05}=1.645.\]

One-sided confidence bounds are commonly used when regulations require demonstrating that a quantity is above or below a specified threshold. Examples include minimum product strength, maximum contaminant concentration, or guaranteed service life.

Example 1

A manufacturer wants to estimate the mean height of adult men. Men's heights are normally distributed with a known population standard deviation of 2.5 inches. A random sample of 50 men has a mean height of 71 inches. Find a 95% upper confidence bound for the population mean height.

Solution

Since the population standard deviation is known, we use a one-sided \(z\) confidence bound for the population mean.

Given

\[\bar{x}=71,\qquad \sigma=2.5,\qquad n=50,\]

the standard error is

\[\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}=\frac{2.5}{\sqrt{50}}\approx0.3536.\]

For a 95% upper confidence bound, the critical value is

\[z_{0.05}=1.645.\]

The upper confidence bound is

\[\bar{x}+z_{0.05}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}=71+1.645(0.3536)\approx71.58.\]

Therefore, the 95% upper confidence bound for the population mean is 71.58 inches. We are 95% confident that \(\mu<71.58\) inches.

The 95% upper confidence bound produced by this procedure is 71.58 inches. In repeated sampling, approximately 95% of similarly constructed upper confidence bounds would exceed the true population mean.

Example 2

80 windshields are inspected at an automotive plant and 74 are found to be acceptable. Find the 99% lower confidence bound of the proportion of windshields that are acceptable.

Solution

Let \(p\) be the true proportion of acceptable windshields.

From the sample,

\[n=80,\qquad x=74,\qquad \hat{p}=\frac{74}{80}=0.925.\]

Since

\[n\hat{p}=74\ge5\quad \text{and}\quad n\hat{q}=6\ge5,\]

the normal approximation may be used.

The standard error is

\[\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}\hat{q}}{n}}=\sqrt{\frac{(0.925)(0.075)}{80}}\approx0.0294.\]

For a 99% lower confidence bound, the critical value is \(z_{0.01}=2.326\).

The lower confidence bound is

\[\hat{p}-z_{0.01}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}\hat{q}}{n}}=0.925-2.326(0.0294)\approx0.8566.\]

Therefore, the 99% lower confidence bound for the true proportion of acceptable windshields is 0.8566.

Assuming the 80 windshields were selected using an appropriate random sampling method, we are 99% confident that at least 85.66% of all windshields produced are acceptable, or more formally, in repeated sampling, approximately 99% of intervals constructed by this procedure will contain the true proportion.

Key Points

  • A confidence interval estimates an unknown population parameter using sample data.
  • The confidence intervals introduced in this chapter have the form \(\text{estimate}\pm\text{margin of error}.\)
  • For a population mean with known \(\sigma\), use a normal-based confidence interval when the sampling distribution of \(\bar{X}\) is approximately normal. This occurs when the population is approximately normal or there is a sufficiently large sample under appropriate conditions.
  • Use Student's \(t\) distribution when the population standard deviation is unknown.
  • Confidence intervals for proportions are based on the sampling distribution of the sample proportion.
  • Confidence bounds provide one-sided estimates when only an upper or lower limit is needed.
  • Holding the confidence level and variability constant, larger samples produce narrower confidence intervals, while higher confidence levels produce wider intervals.
