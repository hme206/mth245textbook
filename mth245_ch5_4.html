Chapter 5: Measures of Center and Spread

Section 5.4: Quantiles

Measures of center, such as the mean and median, summarize the location of a data set. Often, however, we are interested in describing the location of observations within the data. Quantiles divide an ordered data set into equal-sized groups, making it easier to understand the distribution of the data.

The most common quantiles are quartiles, deciles, and percentiles.

Common quantiles
QuantileNumber of Equal Parts
Quartiles4
Deciles10
Percentiles100

To find any quantile, first arrange the observations from smallest to largest.

Quartiles and Deciles

Definitions: Quartiles and Deciles

Three quartiles divide the data into four approximately equal groups.

Nine deciles divide an ordered data set into ten equal parts.

Example 1

Consider the following set of sorted test scores:

\[74,\ 77,\ 77,\ 80,\ 87,\ 87,\ 88,\ 88,\ 88,\ 89,\ 89,\ 89,\ 89,\ 90,\ 90,\ 91,\ 91,\ 92,\ 92,\ 96\]

Divide the data into four equal portions separated by three quartiles, and into ten equal portions separated by nine deciles.

Solution

Since there are 20 observations, we can graphically divide this into four equal portions with three quartiles separating them:

\[74\ 77\ 77\ 80\ 87\ |\ 87\ 88\ 88\ 88\ 89\ |\ 89\ 89\ 89\ 90\ 90\ |\ 91\ 91\ 92\ 92\ 96\]

We can also graphically divide this into ten equal portions with nine deciles separating them:

\[74\ 77\ |\ 77\ 80\ |\ 87\ 87\ |\ 88\ 88\ |\ 88\ 89\ |\ 89\ 89\ |\ 89\ 90\ |\ 90\ 91\ |\ 91\ 92\ |\ 92\ 96\]

Percentiles

Definition: Percentiles

Percentiles divide an ordered data set into one hundred equal parts.

The \(p\)th percentile is a value below which approximately \(p\%\) of the observations fall. For example,

  • the 25th percentile is approximately \(Q_1\),
  • the 50th percentile is the median,
  • the 75th percentile is approximately \(Q_3\).

Different textbooks and statistical software use different conventions for calculating percentiles, especially for small data sets. This text uses the following convention: since a value cannot fall below itself, no value can be interpreted as being in the 100th percentile.

Percentiles are widely used in education, medicine, and standardized testing.

Beyond finding the values for quartiles, a common question is to determine the quartile, decile, or percentile of a given data point in a set.

Example 2

Consider the set of sorted test scores:

\[52,\ 60,\ 71,\ 71,\ 74,\ 77,\ 77,\ 80,\ 87,\ 87,\ 88,\ 88,\ 88,\ 89,\ 89,\ 89,\ 89,\ 90,\ 90,\ 91,\ 91,\ 92,\ 92,\ 96\]

Identify the quartile, decile, and percentile for the score of 92.

Solution

In this set we have 24 values. The score of 92 is higher than 21 of the values, so \(\frac{21}{24}=0.875\).

  • 4th quartile because \(0.875>0.75\).
  • 9th decile because \(0.8<0.875<0.9\).
  • 88th percentile because \(0.87<0.875<0.88\).

Quartiles and the Five-Number Summary

As we mentioned earlier, the three quartiles divide the data into four approximately equal groups. Mathematically, each quartile is the limit or value that divides the regions in parts.

The Median-of-Halves Method

This text uses the “median of halves” method to calculate quartiles.

  • \(Q_1\) (first quartile) is the median of the lower half of the data.
  • \(Q_2\) (second quartile) is the median of the entire data set.
  • \(Q_3\) (third quartile) is the median of the upper half of the data.

Example 3

Find the quartiles of the following set of sorted test scores:

\[57,\ 65,\ 72,\ 73,\ 74,\ 78,\ 79,\ 80,\ 82,\ 82,\ 84,\ 84,\ 86,\ 87,\ 88,\ 89,\ 89,\ 90,\ 91,\ 92,\ 93,\ 94,\ 95,\ 98.\]

Solution

Since there are 24 observations,

\[Q_2=\frac{84+86}{2}=85.\]

The lower half consists of the first 12 observations

\[57,\ 65,\ 72,\ 73,\ 74,\ 78,\ 79,\ 80,\ 82,\ 82,\ 84,\ 84\]

whose median is

\[Q_1=\frac{78+79}{2}=78.5.\]

The upper half consists of the last 12 observations

\[86,\ 87,\ 88,\ 89,\ 89,\ 90,\ 91,\ 92,\ 93,\ 94,\ 95,\ 98\]

whose median is

\[Q_3=\frac{90+91}{2}=90.5.\]

Thus,

\[Q_1=78.5,\qquad Q_2=85,\qquad Q_3=90.5.\]

We can show this graphically as

\[57\ 65\ 72\ 73\ 74\ 78\ |\ 79\ 80\ 82\ 82\ 84\ 84\ |\ 86\ 87\ 88\ 89\ 89\ 90\ |\ 91\ 92\ 93\ 94\ 95\ 98\]
