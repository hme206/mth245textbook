Chapter 9: Hypothesis Testing

Section 9.1: Introduction

Overview

Hypothesis testing is a statistical method used to evaluate claims about a population parameter using sample data. By formulating null and alternative hypotheses, selecting a significance level, and analyzing an appropriate test statistic or \(p\)-value, we can determine whether the sample provides sufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis in favor of the alternative hypothesis. This chapter introduces two-tailed, left-tailed, and right-tailed tests, Type I and Type II errors, statistical power, and hypothesis tests for population means and proportions.

Learning Objectives

After completing this chapter, students should be able to:

  • Define the null and alternative hypotheses.
  • Explain Type I and Type II errors.
  • Describe the relationship among significance level, power, and sample size.
  • Perform hypothesis tests for a population mean and a population proportion.
  • Apply both the traditional (critical value) method and the \(p\)-value method.
  • Interpret the conclusion of a hypothesis test in the context of the problem.
